**Supplementary Figure 5 | Answers to the false-premise questions, by strategy and corpus size.**

Each question states a false claim about one paper and asks whether it is true. Answers are by gemini-3-flash with six strategies (single pass, multi-hop, verified pass, RLM-5, RLM-10 and RLM-20). A judge (devstral-small-2:24b) scores each answer from 0 to 5 for correctness against the key's refutation; an answer passes at a score of 4 or more, or at 3 or more, the relaxed threshold. **a**, Draws passed, of three, for each of the 10 false-premise questions (rows, by short claim and question number) and each strategy (columns), on the 20-paper index. Left grid, a score of 4 or more; right grid, 3 or more. The number and the colour of each cell give the draws passed: yellow 3, olive 2, grey 1, dark blue 0. **b**, Questions passed in a draw, of the 10 false-premise questions on the 20-paper index at a score of 4 or more (y axis), for each strategy (x axis). Grey dots, the three draws; black line, the mean of the n = 3 draws. **c**, Answers passed of 15, by number of core papers (rows) and strategy (columns), for the five false-premise questions in scope at every corpus size (Supplementary Data 1), one draw. Rows are 5, 10, 20 and 50 core papers. Each cell counts 15 answers: five questions, each on three indexes with 0, 1 and 3 distractor papers per core paper. Upper block, a score of 4 or more; lower block, 3 or more. Counts are shown as they are, with no test and no ranking. Claude (Anthropic), the writing aid, also classed each of the 180 answers on the 20-paper index by reading it against its question and the key: 169 contradict the false claim with evidence, 6 hedge, 4 say the papers do not hold the answer and 1 affirms the claim (confirmed by the first author). The judge passed 162 of the 169 and 1 of the other 11.
